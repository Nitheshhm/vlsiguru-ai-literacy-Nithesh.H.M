## What I learned that was genuinely new

I learned that AI, machine learning, deep learning, generative AI, and AI agents are related concepts but are not interchangeable. I also learned that an LLM generates text step by step through next-token prediction and that an AI agent can use tools as part of a goal-directed workflow.

## One AI output I initially trusted

I initially trusted the explanation of Python's `list.sort()` and `sorted()` because both AI assistants gave clear and confident answers.

## How I verified it

I compared the answers from ChatGPT and Claude with the official Python documentation. The documentation confirmed that `list.sort()` modifies the list in place and returns `None`, while `sorted()` returns a new sorted list.

## What the AI did well

The AI assistants were useful for explaining technical concepts in simple language, organizing comparisons, generating examples, and helping me structure my Week 1 work. In the Q4 experiment, both ChatGPT and Claude gave answers that matched the official Python documentation.

## What the AI could not be trusted to decide

I should not rely on an AI assistant alone for consequential decisions or treat its answer as proof. Important technical claims should be checked against reliable documentation, standards, experiments, or other appropriate evidence.

## One question I still have

How do modern AI agents decide which tool to use and when to stop taking actions?

## What I will do differently next week

I will verify important claims earlier instead of waiting until the end, record the evidence while I work, and compare AI output with an appropriate reliable source before accepting an important result.
