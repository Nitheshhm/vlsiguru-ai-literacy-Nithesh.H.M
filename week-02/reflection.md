# Week 2 — Reflection
## 1. What I Learned
This week, I learned the foundational concepts of machine learning, including the relationship between AI, ML, deep learning, generative AI, and large language models.

I learned how datasets, samples, features, and labels are used in machine learning. I also understood the differences between supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning.

Another important learning was how models are trained and used for inference, and how their performance can be evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, and confusion matrices.

## 2. Most Important Insight
My most important insight is that a machine learning model is only as useful as its data, evaluation, and suitability for the problem.

A model may perform well on training data but fail on unseen data. Therefore, evaluating generalization, checking data quality, and understanding the risks of incorrect predictions are essential.

## 3. Connection to Physical Design
As a Physical Design learner, I can see how machine learning might help estimate timing-related outcomes or identify designs that need further investigation.

For example, a model could learn from historical design characteristics and timing results to predict whether a new design is likely to meet timing.

However, such predictions would need careful evaluation and should support engineering decisions rather than replace timing analysis or sign-off.

## 4. Challenges Faced
One challenge was distinguishing between similar machine learning concepts, such as an algorithm and a model, or training and inference.

Understanding evaluation metrics and the consequences of false positives and false negatives also required careful attention.

Creating examples related to semiconductor Physical Design helped me connect the concepts to my learning track.

## 5. What I Want to Learn Next
I want to learn how to use Python to explore datasets, prepare features, train a basic machine learning model, and evaluate its predictions.

I also want to understand how machine learning can be applied responsibly to practical semiconductor engineering problems.

## Conclusion
Week 2 helped me build a foundation in machine learning concepts, evaluation, and risk awareness. I now have a clearer understanding of when machine learning may be useful and why its predictions must be evaluated carefully before being used in engineering workflows.
